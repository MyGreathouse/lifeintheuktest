Life in the UK Test: Government Questions

Government and law questions are consistently among the most-missed on the Life in the UK Test, because they involve specific structural detail: how Parliament works, the difference between the Crown Court and Magistrates' Court, and who is responsible for what.

Study the government chapters

We split this topic into two study chapters — Government and Democracy, and The Law and Your Role — so you can focus on structure first, then civic responsibilities.

Government and Democracy

Parliament, the House of Commons and House of Lords, general elections, the role of the Prime Minister, the monarchy, and devolution to Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

The Law and Your Role

The court system, the police, jury service, driving law, income tax and National Insurance, local councils, and how to take part in local democracy.

Sample questions

Minor criminal cases in England and Wales are usually heard in:

Correct answer: B) The Magistrates' Court

Most minor criminal cases are heard by magistrates, who are usually unpaid volunteers from the local community.

The Northern Ireland Assembly meets at:

Correct answer: B) Stormont

The Northern Ireland Assembly meets at Stormont in Belfast.

Who is the head of state of the UK?

Correct answer: B) The monarch

The monarch is head of state, while the Prime Minister is head of government.

Which court in England and Wales deals with the most serious criminal cases?

Correct answer: B) The Crown Court

Serious criminal cases in England and Wales are tried in the Crown Court before a judge and jury.

Cases involving personal injury, debt and divorce in England and Wales are usually heard in:

Correct answer: B) County Courts

County Courts deal with a wide range of civil disputes including debt, personal injury, contract and family matters.

Jury service in the UK is generally compulsory for eligible people aged:

Correct answer: B) 18 to 75

Those aged 18 to 75 who are on the electoral register and meet residency requirements may be called for jury service.
